## Abstract

Distribution-free conformal selection offers lesion detectors a *precision dial*: select candidate
lesions so that the false-discovery rate (FDR) is at most α, with no assumption on the segmentation
network. Whether that guarantee survives multiple sclerosis (MS) MRI — different scanners and
populations, different segmenters, candidates clustered within scans, and segmenters that have
seen part of the evaluation data during training — has not been characterized. We study conformal
lesion selection (conformal p-values from false-candidate calibration scores + Benjamini–Hochberg)
with FLAMeS on MSLesSeg (115 scans, external to FLAMeS), on the Ljubljana cohort — of which we show
that FLAMeS trained on 20 of 30 scans, identified exactly from the released model files, leaving 10
clean scans — and on 30 healthy controls, and with LST-AI on ISBI-2015. Using one-to-one lesion
matching, subject-level resampling and scan-clustered tests, we find that (i) on clean data,
marginal lesion-FDR control is consistent with transport between MSLesSeg and the 10 held-out
Ljubljana scans (mean per-scan FDP ≤ α in every cell; pooled micro-FDR 0.138/0.095/0.111 at
α = 0.3/0.2/0.1 on the 10 scans and 0.170/0.154/0.086 in the reverse direction), but with 10 scans
the intervals reach 0.21; a precision-matched global threshold fails
under the prevalence change (FDR 0.35, 0.35, 0.20 at α = 0.3, 0.2, 0.1) yet transports in the other
direction, so its inferiority is direction-dependent; (ii) the guarantee is only **marginal**: 4–17 % of MSLesSeg
scans exceed the target false-discovery proportion; (iii) **specificity degrades** under
population shift — an MSLesSeg-calibrated null yields 87–173 false detections per 100 healthy scans
(17–25 of 30 scans affected) versus 10–27 (3–7 of 30) under the controls' own null — and **power
collapses** under a segmenter change; (iv) false-candidate scores carry a significant **scan-level
random effect** (ICC 0.094 on MSLesSeg, p = 0.0002). We therefore calibrate per-scan
false-discovery exceedance at the level of the scan: an exact lattice-path envelope under the
conformal rank law, repaired by split-conformal calibration on the simultaneous event. In a
pre-registered evaluation its simultaneous curve was violated in 4.1 % of draws on MSLesSeg (95 %
interval over distinct scans 1.3–7.6 %) and 5.0 % on healthy controls (0–12 %), against δ = 10 %, and
it yields
**certified dissemination in space**: no healthy control was ever certified as DIS — 0 of 30
distinct scans (exact one-sided 95 % upper bound 9.5 %; 0 of the 5 scans where a false DIS reading
was possible, upper bound 45 %; the procedure abstained on every healthy scan) — whereas the raw
segmentation met the brain DIS pattern on 5 of the 30. All selection runs at low lesion-level recall
(selection recall ≤ 0.30), the honest cost of guaranteed
precision at a lesion-level AUROC of 0.70–0.75 on the clean cohorts.

## 1. Introduction

Automated MS lesion segmentation has reached voxel Dice ≈ 0.7–0.75 on external data with
nnU-Net-class models (e.g. FLAMeS), yet deployed detectors still over-segment: our legacy
thesis-era model had lesion-level precision ≈ 0.22 at sensitivity 0.85 (internal benchmark, not
re-verified here). For a radiologist, the operationally relevant quantity is not Dice but *how many
of the flagged lesions are false*. Conformal selection [Jin & Candès 2023] turns any scoring model
into a selector with a finite-sample FDR guarantee, provided the false candidates of a new scan are
exchangeable with a calibration set of false candidates. That proviso is exactly what MS MRI
stresses: acquisition differs across sites, lesion load differs across populations, the segmenter
may change, false candidates inside one scan are not independent draws — and public MS cohorts are
routinely part of the training data of the very segmenters evaluated on them.

This paper does not propose a new conformal method. The selection wrapper is a faithful application
of conformal p-values and Benjamini–Hochberg; the per-scan procedure in §5 builds on exact
simultaneous FDP bounds for conformal p-values [Gazin, Blanchard & Roquain 2024; Song, Jin & Candès
2026]. Our contribution is an **honest, reproducible characterization** of where the guarantee
holds and where it fails on real MS data, with the statistical safeguards such a claim requires —
including a check of which evaluation scans the segmenter was trained on — and a per-scan procedure
that remains valid under the scan clustering we measure.

**Contributions.**
1. A training-overlap audit: for each segmenter × cohort we state whether the cohort was training
   data, with the source; for FLAMeS × Ljubljana we identify the exact 20 training scans from the
   released nnU-Net files (a reusable forensic recipe), which turns a contaminated cohort into a
   10-scan clean one.
2. A characterization of marginal lesion-FDR control along three shift axes — acquisition and
   population shift with the same segmenter (now only on clean data: MSLesSeg ↔ 10 held-out
   Ljubljana scans), zero-prevalence population shift (healthy controls), and segmenter shift —
   with one-to-one lesion matching, subject-level bootstrap intervals and scan-clustered
   permutation tests, against a precision-matched threshold, weighted conformal selection and
   three scan-aware baselines.
3. Evidence that marginal control is not per-scan control.
4. Measurement of a significant scan-level random effect in false-candidate scores, which
   invalidates candidate-level exchangeability, and a per-scan false-discovery-exceedance (FDX)
   procedure whose validity rests on scan-level exchangeability instead [§5].
5. A negative-results record (§6.3): the claims we made and then retracted, including the two
   fatal errors of our own v0.1 draft, kept because they are the likely mistakes of anyone
   repeating this analysis.

## 2. Related work

**Conformal selection and FDR.** Conformal p-values with a shared calibration set are
positively regression dependent on a subset (PRDS), so Benjamini–Hochberg on them controls FDR
[Bates et al. 2023]; Jin & Candès [2023] cast selection by prediction in this framework. Covariate
shift can be handled by weighted conformal p-values [Tibshirani et al. 2019] and weighted conformal
selection, at the cost of estimated likelihood ratios and — for selection — the loss of the
unweighted PRDS argument.

**Grouped (hierarchical) data.** When observations come in exchangeable groups, Dunn, Wasserman &
Ramdas [2023] calibrate at the group level, e.g. by subsampling one observation per group. Lee &
Ren [2025, arXiv 2501.02514] build subsampling and hierarchical (1/N_k-weighted) conformal
e-values for selection from hierarchical data and control the batch FDR with e-BH; their guarantee
additionally requires group sizes to be independent of the observations, which is doubtful for MS
scans (the number of candidates tracks lesion load). Conformal risk control [Angelopoulos et al.
2024] and Learn-then-Test [Angelopoulos et al. 2021, *verify venue*] control the expectation (or a
high-probability bound) of any bounded loss over exchangeable units; with the scan as unit and the
per-scan FDP as loss they give scan-level FDR control for a global threshold. We use all three as
baselines (§4.6).

**Simultaneous FDP bounds.** Post hoc bounds on the false-discovery proportion via reference
families and λ-calibration [Blanchard, Neuvial & Roquain 2020], closed-form simultaneous bounds
under independence [Katsevich & Ramdas 2020], the exact (Pólya-urn) joint law of conformal p-values
[Gazin, Blanchard & Roquain 2024], and everywhere-valid FDP bounds for conformal inference [Song,
Jin & Candès 2026] provide the machinery for per-scan (FDX) guarantees. All of them require
exchangeability of the null test scores with the calibration scores *at the level of the individual
candidate*.

**Uncertainty for MS lesion segmentation.** Conformal prediction has been applied to patient-level
MS diagnosis [npj Digital Medicine 2025, verify] and to lesion-level miss-rate (FNR) control in 3D
segmentation [arXiv:2510.17897, verify]; the latter controls the complementary error to ours. Shift
benchmarks for MS segmentation exist (Shifts 2.0), but they are built from MSSEG-2016, ISBI-2015 and
Ljubljana, all of which are FLAMeS training data. To our knowledge no study characterizes
lesion-level FDR guarantees across real scanner/population/segmenter shift while controlling for
the segmenter's training-set overlap.

taken from the cohort-build scripts (`docs/calm-ms/src/vm-flames.sh`, `vm-sibbms.sh`) and the
project log; the cloud-VM metadata of the original run was not re-inspected, which is why the
primary clean set is the 10 scans no fold trained on.

are listed in the Reproducibility section. The maps of the trained patients were produced from the

treat them as contaminated regardless.

*Does contamination visibly change the scores?* Not detectably at this sample size: the
false-candidate score law of the 20 trained scans is not distinguishable from that of the 10
held-out scans (scan-clustered permutation KS, D = 0.088, p = 0.54), and their lesion-level AUROC
is, if anything, lower (0.635 vs 0.698; difference −0.063, 95 % CI −0.140 to 0.029). Absence of a
detectable effect with 10 vs 20 scans is not evidence of absence, and in-distribution data cannot
serve as external evidence whatever its effect size; we therefore exclude the trained scans from
every claim.

## 4. Characterization of marginal lesion-FDR control

**Procedure.** For a test scan with candidate scores s₁…s_m and a calibration set of n false-candidate
scores c₁…c_n (from other subjects of the same site, or from another site), the conformal p-value is
p_j = (1 + #{i : c_i ≥ s_j}) / (n + 1) and candidates are selected by Benjamini–Hochberg at level α
within the scan. BH within a scan bounds that scan's *expected* FDP, so we report the **mean
per-scan FDP** (the guaranteed quantity) next to the pooled **micro-FDR** (false/selected over all
scans) with subject-bootstrap 95 % CIs, the number of distinct scans whose own FDP exceeds α (exact
Clopper–Pearson interval for single-timepoint cohorts), and three lesion-level recalls: *selection*
(denominator: GT lesions that some candidate is matched to), *end-to-end* (all GT ≥ 3 mm³) and
*clinical* (GT ≥ 14.14 mm³, the 3 mm-diameter criterion).

every cell (0.009–0.122). The pooled micro-FDR is at or below α except at α = 0.10, where it is

scans (micro-FDR ≤ α at 0.3 and 0.2, with far higher recall, but more scans above α: 1/10 and 3/10

openms) at α = 0.3/0.2/0.1; our re-analysis reproduces these exactly. They are no longer evidence of
cross-site transport.

**Exchangeability, measured at the scan level.** A scan-clustered permutation test (relabelling
whole scans, 2000 permutations) does not distinguish the false-candidate score law of the 10
held-out scans from MSLesSeg's (KS-D = 0.095, p = 0.56). With 10 scans the test has little power;
"not rejected" is all it says. (v0.1's D = 0.048, p = 0.71 used all 30 Ljubljana scans and is
withdrawn as external evidence; re-run: D = 0.048, p = 0.69.) A naive candidate-level KS test is
invalid under within-scan clustering.

### 4.2 Marginal is not per-scan

Even where the mean per-scan FDP is ≤ α, 4–17 % of MSLesSeg scans exceed α (table above:
leave-one-subject-out 7.0–14.8 %, held-out-calibrated 4.3–16.5 %), and 0–2 of the 10 held-out
Ljubljana scans. An empirically calibrated per-scan operating point — the largest α whose own-site

### 4.4 Segmenter shift

A FLAMeS-calibrated null — MSLesSeg only, or the 10 held-out Ljubljana scans — applied to
LST-AI-segmented ISBI selects nothing at any α: LST-AI scores lie lower (false 0.75, true 0.81), so
no candidate clears the FLAMeS null. This is a collapse of power, the FDR-safe direction. ISBI
confounds segmenter, acquisition and population, so we do not attribute the effect to the segmenter
alone.

### 4.5 Scan clustering

Grouping false candidates by scan (one-to-one labels, scans with ≥ 2 false candidates), the
one-way intraclass correlation of their scores is 0.094 on MSLesSeg (112 scans, 1346 false); the
between-scan mean square is 2.04× its mean under exchangeable reshuffling of false scores across
scans (5000 permutations, p = 0.0002). On the 10 held-out Ljubljana scans (8 with ≥ 2 false
candidates, 93 false) ICC = 0.19 (2.95×, p = 0.003); on all 30 (in-distribution) 0.078 (2.20×,
p = 0.0002). Candidates of one scan are therefore not exchangeable draws from the pooled
false-candidate law — the assumption under which the joint law of conformal p-values, and every
simultaneous FDP bound built on it, is exact.

## 5. Per-scan false-discovery exceedance control under scan clustering

A radiologist reads one scan. The target is therefore false-discovery *exceedance* control per scan,
P(FDP_scan > γ) ≤ δ, and — since clinical decisions are made per anatomical area — simultaneous
lower bounds on the number of true lesions in any region, with **certified dissemination in space**
as the clinically meaningful consequence.

### 5.1 Layer 1: an exact envelope under the conformal rank law

For a scan with m candidates, write k_j = (n+1)p_j ∈ {1,…,n+1} for the conformal ranks and let
V_k (R_k) be the number of false (all) candidates with rank ≤ k. If the calibration false scores
and the scan's m₀ false scores are jointly exchangeable (assumption A1), (V_1,…,V_{n+1}) follows a
universal law P_{n,m₀}: all C(n+m₀, m₀) interleavings of calibration and false test scores are
equally likely [Gazin, Blanchard & Roquain 2024]; the true candidates never enter a false
candidate's p-value. We fix a non-decreasing integer envelope G^{(r)} per candidate null count r —
a truncated higher-criticism shape G_k = min{r, max_{l≤k} ⌊r t_l + λ √(r t_l(1−t_l)(1+r/n))⌋} on
t_k = k/(n+1) ≤ 0.5 (pre-registered on synthetic data only; a Simes-type template and an
HC∧Simes hybrid are reported as secondary) — and choose λ as the smallest value whose **exact**
crossing probability P_{n,r}(∃k: V_k > G_k) is ≤ δ. The crossing probability is computed exactly by
a dynamic program over the anti-diagonals of the lattice path (state = calibration and test points
consumed; a state is killed when the tests seen before the (i+1)-th calibration point exceed
G_{i+1}); it matches brute-force enumeration of all interleavings to 10⁻¹² and carries no Monte
Carlo error. Unknown m₀ is handled by the completion coupling (adding null test points can only
increase V) and an m₀-adaptive closure over a geometric grid of r, and the bound is self-refined
[Song, Jin & Candès 2026; Blanchard, Neuvial & Roquain 2020]. On the single event
E = {V_k ≤ G^{(r*)}_k ∀k} (probability ≥ 1−δ) this yields B*_k ≥ V_k for all k at once, so the
selection "largest k with B*_k ≤ γR_k" has FDP ≤ γ, and — by interpolation — for **any** subset S
of candidates, #false(S) ≤ min_k(|S ∩ {rank > k}| + B*_k).

A closed-form alternative is not available: the Katsevich–Ramdas constant, derived under
independence, crosses its envelope with probability > δ = 0.1 under the conformal dependence (exact
computation; a regression test pins this at n = 200, r = 100 and passes in v0.2; the range
0.118–0.136 quoted in v0.1 is not re-verified).

### 5.2 Layer 1 is not valid on MS data

A1 is a statement about candidates, and it is false for MS MRI: false-candidate scores share a
scan-level component (§4.5; exact permutation tests of cross-scan exchangeability reject on
MSLesSeg and on the 10 held-out Ljubljana scans, §5.6). In the tight regime (few false candidates,
strong signal) Layer 1 has no slack, and a scan random effect of the measured size pushes realized
FDX above δ [§5.6, synthetic study]. We therefore report Layer 1 on real data as **empirical only**.

### 5.3 Layer 2: scan-level calibration on the simultaneous event

We index a nested family by an integer inflation c ≥ 0 added to every envelope (B* is
non-decreasing in c, so selections shrink and bounds loosen as c grows). For each of K labelled
subjects (one random scan each) we compute the smallest c such that the scan's true null counts
satisfy V_k ≤ B*_k(c) for **all** k — the simultaneous event, not merely the FDP of one selection —
and set ĉ to the ⌈(K+1)(1−δ)⌉-th smallest value (abstain if that index exceeds K). If the K labelled
subjects and the test subject are exchangeable given the calibration pool, the split-conformal
quantile lemma gives P(the test scan's curve is violated at ĉ) ≤ δ, which covers the selected set,
any post-hoc threshold, and every region certificate at once [cf. Dunn, Wasserman & Ramdas 2023].
Candidate-level exchangeability is no longer required. The pool may come from another site: only
the labelled and test subjects must be exchangeable. (Calibrating Layer 2 on the selected set's FDP
alone — an earlier design — covers only that selection and not the region certificates.) The
guarantee is marginal over the draw of labelled and test subjects; an empirical check therefore
estimates P(violation) over distinct test scans, and repeated splits of the same scans add Monte
Carlo precision about the split, not new observations (§5.6).

### 5.4 Certified dissemination in space

Each candidate is assigned a MAGNIMS area with the application's own atlas-based zone map (LST-AI
MSMask, periventricular / juxtacortical / infratentorial / deep white matter; per-lesion cascade
IT > PV > JC, else DWM). For the brain McDonald areas a ∈ {PV, JC, IT}, the certified true-lesion
count is L_a = |S_a| − min(|S_a|, min_k(|S_a ∩ {rank > k}| + B*_k)). DIS is **certified** when
L_a ≥ 1 in at least two areas; on the good event every certified area truly contains a true lesion,
so P(a certified DIS is false) ≤ δ. We compare with DIS read off the raw segmentation and off the
BH-selected candidates, on patients and — decisively — on healthy controls, where every DIS call is
false.

### 5.5 Pre-registration

Template, γ = 0.2, δ = 0.1, grids, labels and splits were fixed and committed to version control
before the real-data run (commit c47c88d). One amendment (v2) was made before any Layer-2 real-data
output was observed, after the adversarial validity review: Layer 2 moved to the simultaneous event
and to pools from other sources; Layer 1 was demoted to empirical. The amendment and its reasons
are recorded verbatim in the evaluation script. **Post-hoc deviations in v0.2 (not pre-registered,
made after seeing v0.1 results, for the reasons in the changelog):** (i) results are aggregated per
distinct scan; (ii) the healthy-control pool is rebuilt from MSLesSeg only (the pre-registered pool
included FLAMeS training data) — both pools are reported on the same pre-registered splits; (iii)
the Ljubljana evaluation is restricted to the 10 FLAMeS-held-out scans, where the pre-registered
K = 20 / T = 10 split is impossible, so Layer 2 is run by rotation (each held-out scan is the test
scan once, the other 9 are the labelled scans; ⌈10 · 0.9⌉ = 9 ≤ 9, so ĉ is the largest of the 9
safe values).

## 6. Discussion

### 6.1 What a deployer can rely on
Within a site and a segmenter, marginal lesion-FDR control is reliable; whether it transports to a
different site is supported on clean data only by 10 held-out scans of one other site, which is
consistent with transport but cannot establish it. Per-scan control — what a clinician reading one
scan needs — requires calibrating at the level of the scan: candidate-level guarantees assume away a
scan random effect that is present in MS MRI. With scan-level calibration, the certificate a deployer
can rely on is one-sided: a *certified* DIS or region count is wrong with probability ≤ δ by
construction; empirically no healthy control was certified (0/30 distinct scans, upper bound 9.5 %;
the procedure abstained on all of them), whereas uncertified readings of the same segmentation call
DIS in one healthy scan in six (5/30, 95 % CI 6–35 %). The empirical bound is loose, and on the 5 healthy scans where a false call was
possible it is looser still (0/5, upper bound 45 %): the certificate's protection on healthy subjects
rests on the theorem and the exchangeability it assumes, not on our 30 controls. It is not a
detector: most scans receive no certificate. Calibration must be redone with labelled subjects from
the target population when the population (e.g. screening of healthy subjects) or the segmenter
changes; with the segmenter the failure mode is silence (no selections), not false reassurance.
Finally, a deployer must check that the evaluation data were not the segmenter's training data —
public MS benchmarks frequently are.

### 6.2 The cost: recall
At the score quality of current models (lesion-level AUROC 0.70–0.75 on the clean cohorts, Table 3),
guaranteed precision is bought with low lesion-level recall (on clean data, selection recall ≤ 0.30
and end-to-end recall ≤ 0.23 at α = 0.3; end-to-end 0.02–0.04 at α = 0.1; §4.1). The
guarantee makes the trade-off explicit; it does not remove it. Better scores, not better conformal
wrappers, move this frontier.

### 6.3 Claims we retracted (negative-results record)
From before v0.1:
1. *"Conformal lesion-FDR breaks across MS site shift."* Refuted by our own same-segmenter axis.
2. *"The guarantee transports despite non-exchangeability."* The non-exchangeability was an artifact
   of a candidate-level KS test and many-to-one matching.
3. *"44–95 % of scans exceed α."* An exceedance denominator restricted to scans that selected
   something; over all scans the figure is 4–17 % (§4.2).

From v0.1 (corrected in v0.2):
4. *"Marginal lesion-FDR control transports across same-segmenter patient cohorts (permutation
   p = 0.71)."* Two-thirds of the second cohort was the segmenter's training data. Withdrawn as
   external evidence; the clean replacement (10 scans) is consistent with transport but cannot
   establish it.
5. *"Conformal selection outperforms a precision-matched global threshold, which fails to
   transport."* True only in the direction with a prevalence change; in the other clean direction
   the threshold transports with higher recall (but more scans above α).
6. *"A per-scan operating point transfers from the more to the less heterogeneous site."* On clean
   data: 0/10 exceedances, exact upper bound 31 % — not established.
7. *"0 of 200 healthy scans certified as DIS (95 % CI ≤ 1.8 %)"* and *"raw segmentation met the DIS
   pattern in 40/200"*: repeated splits counted as independent scans. Distinct-scan figures: 0/30
   (upper bound 9.5 %) and 5/30.
8. *"A patient null produces 7–13× the floor"* used contaminated nulls; with a clean null 6.5–8.7×.
9. *"Candidate-level AUC ≈ 0.75–0.82"* had no source in our records; measured 0.70–0.75 on the
   clean cohorts.
Each retraction came from an independent review of code and claims; items 4–9 are pinned by the
v0.2 scripts and records.

### 6.4 Limitations
Only one clean acquisition-shift comparison exists, and its second site has 10 scans; a segmenter
trained without any of our evaluation cohorts (e.g. the self-trained nnU-Net of
`research/nnunet/`) or a FLAMeS-independent multi-site set (MSSEG-2, MICCAI-2008) is needed to make
a transport claim. The training-overlap identification for FLAMeS × Ljubljana is exact for the
released weights; for the private FLAMeS sources overlap with our cohorts is excluded only by the
paper's description. SibBMS licensing is self-contradictory in its paper (CC BY 3.0 vs CC BY-NC-SA
3.0) and the ISBI scans' provenance (which 19 of 21, which rater) must be confirmed. Thirty healthy
controls bound the false-certification rate only at 9.5 %; about 300 controls with zero false
certifications would be needed to bound it at 1 %. The per-scan guarantee of §5 is marginal over the calibration draw, not
conditional on one calibration set. Several §4–§5 analyses (distinct-scan aggregation, clean pools,
rotation design on the held-out scans) are post-hoc deviations from the pre-registration.
