01
Motivation, from our own data

The real failure mode isn't missing lesions — it's inventing them

Every MS segmenter is graded on Dice. But clinicians abandon tools for a different reason: false positives. Our benchmark makes this concrete. The AI reaches 0.85 sensitivity (it detects real lesions) but only 0.22 lesion-wise precision — roughly four of every five "lesions" it reports are spurious. That destroys trust, inflates apparent lesion load, and corrupts the very DIS/atrophy metrics the app computes downstream.

The state of the art chases a higher mean Dice. That is the wrong target for a clinical product. A tool a neurologist will actually use must let them choose an operating point — "show me only lesions you are 90% sure of" — and it must be able to keep that promise. No published MS segmenter does this with a statistical guarantee. That gap is our opening.

03
The thesis

Unite the dispersed advances into one trustworthy, controllable segmenter

Foundation models are powerful but uncalibrated. Domain generalization travels alone. Uncertainty work rarely reaches the lesion level. Biomarker models need contrasts most sites lack. Nobody has fused them into a segmenter a clinician can trust and tune.

Central contribution

Conformal, lesion-wise risk control. We wrap any base segmenter in a calibration layer that turns per-lesion uncertainty into a distribution-free statistical guarantee on the lesion-level false-discovery rate. The clinician sets a target FDR (say, ≤10%); conformal prediction guarantees it holds on unseen data. The over-segmentation problem becomes a controllable, provable dial. Conformal risk control is a top-tier ML trend — and it has never been applied to MS lesion segmentation.

04
The system

CALM-MS architecture

A staged pipeline: a domain-generalized backbone feeds lesion candidates with uncertainty; a conformal layer converts that uncertainty into a guaranteed operating point; longitudinal and biomarker heads extend it; and the app's expert corrections feed a data flywheel back into training.

STAGE 1

Generalize across scanners

Backbone trained with Random-Convolution augmentation + intensity harmonization so it holds on unseen sites — the field's #1 deployment blocker.

STAGE 2

Score every lesion

Instance-aware candidates with a calibrated per-lesion confidence, not just a voxel probability — the unit clinicians reason in.

STAGE 3

Guarantee the operating point

Conformal calibration converts confidence into a provable lesion-level FDR bound. The precision dial the product needs, with the math a journal wants.

06
Experimental design

How we prove it

Datasets

ISBI-2015 (multi-rater, longitudinal) · MSSEG-2 · MSLesSeg-2025 (new public set) · our expert-annotated cohort. Multi-site for the domain-generalization claim; leave-one-site-out for unseen-scanner tests.

Baselines

nnU-Net, LST-AI, the legacy AI, and an uncalibrated version of our own model — so the conformal guarantee is isolated as the contribution.

Metrics

Dice, Jaccard, AVD; surface HD95, ASSD; lesion-wise LTPR / LFPR / F1 (18-conn, 3 mm³ floor); calibration (ECE, reliability); realized vs target FDR.

Statistics

Per-case bootstrap 95% CIs; Wilcoxon signed-rank across methods; size-stratified detection; coverage validation of the conformal bound on held-out sites.

The headline experiment

Set a target lesion-FDR of 10%. Show that across every backbone and every unseen site, realized FDR stays ≤10% while sensitivity degrades gracefully — turning today's uncontrolled 0.22 precision into a guaranteed, clinician-chosen number. That single plot is the paper.

07
Feasibility

Data & compute — honestly

The conformal core is cheap: it calibrates on top of a trained model with a held-out set of a few dozen cases — we already have the expert reference and the harness. The backbone and domain-generalization training is the real cost.

Compute: single modern GPU (24–48 GB) suffices for nnU-Net-scale training and all conformal work; multi-GPU only accelerates the foundation-model backbone. Data: public sets cover training and external validation; our cohort anchors the clinical-reference claim. Not blocked — unlike the biomarker head, which needs SWI/EPI we don't yet hold.

08
The product, at max level

Because we are a software, not a paper mill

The research only matters if it ships. CALM-MS is designed so every scientific contribution is also a product feature — the paper and the app are the same artifact seen from two sides.

A precision dial in the UI

A slider: "false-positive tolerance." It maps directly to the conformal FDR target. The clinician moves it; the guarantee moves with it. Nothing else on the market offers this.

Per-lesion confidence overlay

Each lesion carries a calibrated confidence, rendered as opacity/colour. Low-confidence lesions are flagged for review, not silently included in DIS/atrophy math.

Trust report for Class C

Every run emits calibration + coverage evidence — auditable proof the operating point held. Regulatory gold for an IEC 62304 device.

The data flywheel

Expert corrections made in the app become active-learning labels. The model that ships gets measurably better with use — the moat academia can't replicate without a product.

Positioning & risk

Where it lands, and what could bite

Target venues

Medical Image Analysis, IEEE TMI, NeuroImage, MSARD; MICCAI for the methods core. The "trustworthy AI + real clinical problem + guarantee" framing is exactly their appetite.

Honest risks

Conformal exchangeability assumptions weaken under heavy domain shift — hence the multi-site coverage test is mandatory, not optional. Small expert cohort limits the clinical-claim strength until more sites are added. Biomarker head is data-blocked.
